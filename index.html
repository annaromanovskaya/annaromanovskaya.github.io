About me

I am an astrophysicist specializing in stellar atmospheres, high-resolution spectroscopy, and chemical peculiarities in stars. My primary research interests encompass magnetic chemically peculiar Ap/Bp stars, metallic-line Am/Fm stars, normal O–B–A–F stars, and spectroscopic binary systems.

I received my degree from Moscow State University in 2017. I conducted my PhD research at the Institute of Astronomy of the Russian Academy of Sciences (RAS), completing my dissertation in 2021 and defending it in 2022.

Research topics

A short overview of the directions I work on, with illustrative figures.

01

Stellar atmospheres and chemical abundances

I study the atmospheric parameters and chemical abundances of early-type stars, including magnetic chemically peculiar Ap/Bp stars, metallic-line Am/Fm stars, and normal O–B–A–F stars. My work is based on high-resolution spectroscopy, stellar atmosphere modelling, and synthetic spectrum fitting.

02

Vertical stratification in magnetic chemically peculiar stars

For magnetic chemically peculiar stars, I investigate the vertical stratification of chemical elements in stellar atmospheres. This includes modelling how elements such as Fe, Cr, Ca, and rare-earth elements are distributed with atmospheric depth and how these abundance gradients affect observed spectra and stellar flux distributions.

03

Non-LTE abundance analysis

I am also interested in implementing non-LTE calculations in stellar abundance analysis. This work uses model atoms developed by my colleagues at INASAN and is aimed at improving the reliability of abundance determinations for selected chemical elements.

04

Binary systems and machine-learning methods

I work on the analysis of spectroscopic binary systems, including spectra disentangling and self-consistent analysis of composite spectra. More recently, I have been applying machine-learning methods to determine the fundamental parameters of both components of binary stars directly from observed or synthetic composite spectra.
